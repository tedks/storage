# Contract for SSH hosts serving multiple Storage members v0 (proposed)

**Owner.** Storage remote transport. **Consumers.** SSH/SFTP filesystem adapters, rsync adapters, watcher, concurrency planners, and mount lifecycle.

- Group transport use within one mount by effective endpoint, port, login, authentication, route and host-verification policy. Keep differing or unverifiable policies isolated. Resolving two hostnames to one IP is insufficient evidence of compatibility. A root path, display name or member priority does not create a new host identity.
- Open lazily with one connection attempt in progress for the identity. Lease independent channels for simultaneous operations. Use the smallest configured member channel ceiling and the applicable server-advertised or observed ceiling as the shared limit; each member still respects its own limit. Derive the shared interactive reserve once under the existing reserve policy. A saturated bulk queue cannot consume the reserve. Admit queued bulk requests in arrival order within their class subject to channel availability and cancellation, so further arrivals from a busy member cannot repeatedly overtake a sibling. A child awaiting a channel must honor its operation's cancellation and timeout contract.
- Member identity, root translation, root health, cache keys, repair records and replication destinations stay independent. A valid session plus an inaccessible root is a filesystem failure. A failed session invalidates its generation. A stale lease returned later cannot close a replacement generation.
- The registry coordinates reconnect attempts and reference lifetimes. Closing or failing one filesystem leaves healthy siblings usable. Last release or mount shutdown reclaims owned resources. Do not replay an uncertain mutation to establish connectivity. Pre-fork probes finish before daemonization.
- The future mount-wide opt-in is `ssh_transport = "openssh"`. Omission preserves the existing default provider and credential and route choices. Per-member overrides are unsupported. In OpenSSH mode, every participating SSH/SFTP, watcher and rsync channel uses the same registry-owned master for its effective identity. Use a private mode-0700 directory and owned control socket per identity, an explicitly managed master with persistence disabled, and mux-only children that fail closed if their socket is missing or stale. Ignore any inherited multiplexing socket selection. Require strict host-key checking against configured trust data in this opt-in mode. Shutdown stops the owned master and reclaims its socket; it never stops a user-owned master. Only the registry can open a new authenticated generation after a transport failure.
- Count one steady-state SSH connection per compatible identity for traffic routed through the registry. Separately identify transient reconnects, startup probes and any subprocesses still using another provider. Include watcher and rsync sessions in the server-side count before claiming the mixed case.

Verify with a synthetic sshd and distinct test roots. Check concurrent reads of different bytes at identical relative names, a missing root alongside a healthy root, separate credentials, close/reconnect and foreground progress under sustained bulk traffic. For the OpenSSH experiment, remove or stop the owned master and verify an attach-only request fails with zero new server-side connections before a separately initiated registry reconnect.
